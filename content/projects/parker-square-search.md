This is my attempt at solving an unsolved maths problem. The goal is to find a 3x3 magic square made of nine different perfect squares. A magic square is a grid where every row, column, and diagonal adds up to the same number. I algebraically rewrote the problem so the search code only has to loop over one number, and that code is public.

It is a nerdy side project. I only gave it a few hours and I do not plan to give it more. I have run it on Google Colab for hours at a time. It has few optimisations, so it is just a shot in the dark.

## The problem

Matt Parker made a [Numberphile video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aOT_bG-vWyg) about magic squares made of perfect squares. He showed a square that almost works but fails badly. That failure is now called a "Parker Square", which is a joke name for maths that does not quite work.

People have searched for a real one for years, more so since the video came out, 8 years before I started this. Nobody has found one, and numbers up to 10^19 have been checked. One might not even exist. A paper proved that these squares exist for every grid size of 4x4 and larger, but the 3x3 case remains unsolved.

## How the search works

Brute force searching over nine separate numbers is futile. Working with o3-mini-high and Gemini 2.0 Pro, I reduced the variables down to just one.

- The nine numbers of the square can be written in terms of three variables: a, e, and c.
- Those three variables are rewritten in terms of four more: X, Y, U, and V, so that Y² − 2X² = D and V² − 2U² = D.
- Both are Pell equations. This is a classic type of equation whose solutions can be generated by a standard algorithm. For any value of D, the algorithm gives infinitely many X, Y, U, and V values.
- From X, Y, U, and V, I get a, e, and c. From those three, I get all nine numbers of the square.

So the search only iterates over D, with the magic-square conditions built in. The [README on GitHub](https://github.com/gut-puncture/Parker-Square-Search?tab=readme-ov-file#finding-a-parker-square) has the full derivation.
