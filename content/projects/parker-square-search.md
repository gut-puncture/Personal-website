This is my attempt at solving an unsolved maths problem, which is to find a 3x3 magic square made of nine different perfect squares. A magic square is a grid of numbers in which every row, every column and both diagonals add up to the same total. I rewrote the problem with algebra so that the search code only has to loop over one number, and that code is public.

It is a nerdy side project. I only gave it a few hours, and I do not plan to give it more. I have run the search on Google Colab for hours at a time, but the code has few optimisations, so it is just a shot in the dark.

## The problem

Matt Parker made a [Numberphile video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aOT_bG-vWyg) about magic squares made of perfect squares. In it he showed a square that almost works but fails badly. That failed square is now called a "Parker Square", and the name has become a joke for maths that does not quite work.

People have searched for a real one for years, and the search grew after the video came out, 8 years before I started this project. Nobody has found one yet, even though numbers up to 10^19 have been checked, and it is possible that one does not exist. A paper has proved that such squares exist for every grid size of 4x4 and larger, but the 3x3 case is still unsolved.

## How the search works

A brute force search over nine separate numbers is futile. Working with o3-mini-high and Gemini 2.0 Pro, I therefore reduced the number of variables down to just one.

- The nine numbers of the square can be written in terms of three variables, which are a, e and c.
- Those three variables are then rewritten in terms of four more, which are X, Y, U and V, so that Y² − 2X² = D and V² − 2U² = D.
- Both of these are Pell equations, which are a classic type of equation whose solutions can be generated by a standard algorithm. For any value of D, this algorithm gives infinitely many values of X, Y, U and V.
- From X, Y, U and V I get a, e and c, and from those three I get all nine numbers of the square.

This means that the search only has to step through values of D, and the conditions for a magic square are already built in. The [README on GitHub](https://github.com/gut-puncture/Parker-Square-Search?tab=readme-ov-file#finding-a-parker-square) has the full derivation.
