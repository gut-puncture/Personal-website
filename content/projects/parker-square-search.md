This is my attempt at an unsolved maths problem: a 3×3 magic square (every row, column and diagonal adds up to the same number) made of nine different perfect squares. I rewrote the problem so the search only has to loop over one number, and the code is public.

It is a nerdy side project. I gave it a few hours and do not plan to give it more. I have run it on Google Colab for hours at a time. It has few optimisations, so it is a shot in the dark.

## The problem

Matt Parker made a [Numberphile video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aOT_bG-vWyg) about magic squares of perfect squares. He showed a square that almost works but fails badly. That failure is now called a "Parker Square", a name for maths that does not quite work.

People have searched for a real one for years, more so since the video came out, 8 years before I started this. Nobody has found one, and numbers up to 10^19 have been checked. One may not even exist: a paper has proved such squares exist for every grid of 4×4 and larger, yet the 3×3 case is still unsolved after years of trying.

## How the search works

Brute force over nine numbers is futile and not fun. Working with o3-mini-high and Gemini 2.0 Pro, I reduced the variables algebraically down to one.

- The nine numbers of the square can be written in terms of three variables, a, e and c.
- Those three are rewritten in terms of four more, X, Y, U and V, so that Y² − 2X² = D and V² − 2U² = D.
- Both are Pell equations, a classic type whose solutions can be generated by a standard algorithm. For any value of D, that gives infinitely many X, Y, U and V.
- From X, Y, U and V I get a, e and c, and from those all nine numbers of the square.

So the search only iterates over D, with the magic-square conditions built in. The [README on GitHub](https://github.com/gut-puncture/Parker-Square-Search?tab=readme-ov-file#finding-a-parker-square) has the full derivation.
